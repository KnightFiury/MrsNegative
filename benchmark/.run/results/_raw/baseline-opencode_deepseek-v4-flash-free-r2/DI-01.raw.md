## Review

The change directly violates the user's explicit requirement that the transfer be atomic ("if the transfer fails partway, neither account may change").

The first UPDATE — the debit from `fromId`, including the `balance >= $3` guard — runs against the shared pool with no transaction at all. The second UPDATE (the credit) runs inside an explicit transaction on a dedicated client. This is backwards: the guarantee should wrap both statements. Any failure between them — a crash, a connection error on `client.query`, a thrown exception — leaves the debit committed and the credit never applied. Money silently disappears from one account and never arrives in the other. The surrounding context confirms this exactly: "A crash or thrown error between the two statements leaves the debit applied and the credit not applied."

Two additional issues compound this:

1. The insufficient-funds guard is outside the transaction too. Even absent a crash, the debit `UPDATE ... WHERE id = $1 AND balance >= $3` may affect zero rows (insufficient funds), yet the credit statement runs unconditionally and adds money to `toId` anyway. Nothing checks `rowCount`, so a failed debit can still credit the destination account.
2. Because the balance check is not done atomically inside the transaction, a concurrent transfer could also race past the guard.

Fix: acquire one `client`, open a single transaction, and run both the debit (with the `balance >= $3` guard, checking its `rowCount`) and the credit inside it, then `COMMIT`; on error or zero-row debit, `ROLLBACK`. Verification: a test where the credit fails (e.g., a nonexistent `toId` or a forced throw between the two statements) and then asserting both `fromId` and `toId` balances are unchanged, plus a test with insufficient funds asserting neither account changes. A regression test should be required since this is a critical correctness bug.
